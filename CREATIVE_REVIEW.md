# Creative review — THE WALL, Chapter 1 (by a Fable 5.2 subagent)

Read-only review based on the README, DEVLOG, story/NPC/event code, sign text, 8 screenshots and the 7 reference frames.

## 1. What's working

- **The cross-section establishing shot** carries the premise in one image: warm, cluttered amber tiers below,
  cold, orderly white tiers above. Class reads before a word does.
- **Sign subtitles are the best writing in the game:** "everything that falls, for sale", "we fix what falls",
  "baked fresh-ish", "fair prices, honest glue", "Home?", "order · light · water".
- **Registrar Quill has a real voice:** "Ten. In ink." / "Some of them are just thumbprints. ...It's valid. Don't look so pleased."
- **Water is a better political resource than candy.** The leaking straw, Wren selling "their runoff" and the
  Heights' Reservoir are original and seeded across all three districts.
- **Strong object-to-architecture ideas:** the ruler checkpoint barrier, the guarded Granary jar, the
  jewellery-box Registry, the spool tower and paper-clip bridge, the jar-lid kitchen roof, the glass floor the
  rich look down through.
- **The candy event is systemic, not a cutscene.** The crowd points, rushes and competes, guards ring the core,
  and prices move.

## 2. Weakest ideas and missed opportunities

- **Scale doesn't read at player height.** Too few recognisable giant objects (brand print, barcodes, thumbprints, half-objects looming overhead).
- **The opening throws away the premise.** Mara explains everything at once; the player has no reaction to being shrunk and no past.
- **The choice costs nothing.** Both branches grant a pass and converge; about 36 tokens isn't tempting; Calloway is a stock villain.
- **The giant changes nothing.** It is a benevolent vending machine on a timer with no mood, intent or risk.
- **The Threadrunners and the Salvage Union are job boards** with no stake in the candy. There is no moral grey.
- **The tone is uniformly cosy.** Names (Pip, Odo, Wren, Rook) are stock fantasy-village rather than scrap-dweller coinages.
- **The Heights is the weakest district visually:** blown-out white and generic, where it should feel uncanny and curated.

## 3. Originality check against the reference images

- **Too close:** the title is identical to the source storyline's name. The skeleton matches too: people shrunk by an
  alien child and kept in a tiered habitat, class by level, candy from above, a hoarding regime. The overview shot and the
  string-lit market echo reference frames. The hand is visually distinct, but its role is the same.
- **Genuinely different:** all names, signs and factions, the water politics, the Registry and petition, and none of
  the source's companion animal or themed sets.
- **How to push further:** rename the game; change the keeper (a collector or curator, or an automated caretaker);
  change why people are inside (some chose it, were sold, or were born there); make water and light the politics and
  candy one of several kinds of "fall"; replace the string-light canopy with a distinct light source (a phone screen,
  a nightlight, a fish-tank glow).

## 4. Top 8 improvements (impact for effort)

1. Rename the game and the "candy from the alien" framing (one config line).
2. Rewrite the opening: wake alone, offer two or three reaction choices, let Mara withhold.
3. Give the choice a real cost: debt or a dependant, a bigger Authority offer, an exclusive permit.
4. Make Calloway persuasive: an argument about rationing through famine cycles and the last riot.
5. Vary the giant's interventions through the event system: tapping the glass, switching a lamp off, taking someone.
6. Give the Runners and the Union a stake in each drop: smuggled shards, wanted wrapper foil.
7. Add 10–15 unmistakable printed giant objects at player height to sell the scale.
8. Restyle the Heights as a curated display: labelled specimens, pins, arranged poses.

## 5. Bold ideas for Chapter 2

1. **The Rearrangement:** the giant tidies the cabinet and moves a block of Drip Row onto the Heights shelf overnight.
2. **The Valve:** the petition "succeeds", the Reservoir drains, and the Authority's scarcity turns out to be partly real.
3. **Speaking to the Sky:** the Threadrunners arrange objects at the glass to send the giant messages, and the giant answers.
